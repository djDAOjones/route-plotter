# sweep — every chat on a checkout

<!-- cspell:ignore worktrees -->

An add-on no rule reads (specification 3.21), for the owner's stock
request to review every chat, archive what is done and make this chat
the orchestrator. Listing, messaging and archiving chats are the
app's; every rule binds every chat. It grants no deploy, gate or
start the owner has not given and writes only its lane until he
answers.

1. **List every chat** whose folder is this checkout, one of its
   worktrees or a clone a lane names: the session list without
   `linked`, which shows one family only, its limit past the default
   twenty; then `git worktree list`, for worktrees with no live chat.
   Each: id, title, folder, branch, parent, state, item. A live
   orchestrator comes first: a chat that keeps a notes file or says it
   orchestrates. If another is live, keep it, go on read-only and put
   the handover in the owner's question.
2. **Census Git**, read-only, after `git fetch`: in the main checkout
   and each worktree, commits not on origin's main branch, staged,
   uncommitted and ignored files, and mode changes by
   `git diff --summary` and its `--cached` form; then stashes, the
   gate and Codex locks and their owners' pids, and running gates and
   second-model runs. Pin each by commit or path in the lane.
   Mode changes are listed, restored only on his word.
3. **Read each chat at source**: its lane's status file, last report,
   commits on origin and background jobs. A relayed status is a lead,
   not evidence; what cannot be checked counts as not landed.
4. **One disposition per chat**, in the lane's census with its reason:
   - *archive* — landed, its work committed and pushed as the Push
     line says; gate-free, nothing of its running and no lock its
     own; no question open;
   - *finish* — not landed: closed by the close verb, by its chat or
     the orchestrator;
   - *wait* — running, or waiting on a sibling or a date;
   - *owner* — needs his ruling, the governing rule quoted;
   - *keep* — this chat, the live orchestrator and any running chat,
     never *archive*.

   Leftover settings, folder-trust entries or locks are listed, not
   changed.
5. **Ask once.** The owner's items go in one batched question,
   recommendation first, each naming its chat and rule, with the
   *archive* list for his yes.
6. **Archive** only what he said yes to, each with its sub-chats
   passing the whole *archive* test again just before. Archiving
   leaves sub-chats' worktrees detached, and the app may reuse one.
   Before archiving, and before removing a worktree on his word,
   `git worktree list`, then
   `git -C <worktree> status --porcelain --ignored`: anything only
   there copied out and compared. Remove the node_modules link first,
   then `git worktree remove` without force. Stop at a refusal.
7. **Then hand over.** Follow the plan verb if no phase is open or he
   asks for one; then the orchestrate verb, this chat the
   orchestrator, its notes file seeded from the census. Chats it did
   not start send it no notes: ask each to message it at a close, push
   or block. It starts a chat only on the owner's turn, or on a turn
   the app opens as its sub-chat ends one, straight after his or
   another such, for a step he asked to have in its own session
   (V31-CHAIN-START); never on a peer's message; a refusal stops it.
   Its start notice may hold the current end, which its first
   sub-chat then sees held: say so in the notes; never clear a claim
   to force a start.
